Reimagine is a technical running apparel brand, with fabrics from Japan, Italy and France, made for people who run for pleasure, not to compete. My brief was to design its launch home page: it had to introduce the brand and sell at the same time.

Starting point

The challenge

Brand or sales

The brand didn't exist yet, so the home page had to introduce it and also be its main sales channel. If it focused on selling, it lost its essence. If it only told the story, it didn't sell.

A home page that tells and sells

A home page with a narrative thread, built on three pillars: recognise, prove and invite. Every sales block is paired with a brand block, so purchases come from identification and trust, never pressure.

Brand

Distilling the brand

I started from the values in the brief (intention, refinement, culture and restraint) and narrowed them down to two attributes no competitor combines.

Signature fabrics

What matters is where each fabric comes from.

Attribute 1

Ritual identity

Running as a personal moment, and clothing as part of that ritual.

Attribute 2

The acid test

Without the ritual identity it would be On Running. Without the signature fabrics, Lululemon.

Why they work together

Benchmark

What to take from each reference

I analysed ten brands: some for how they talk about materials, others for how they turn an activity into a philosophy. From each I kept one idea, or something I knew I didn't want.

Aesop

It always says where each ingredient comes from. I do the same with fabrics.

✓ I take it

Aman Resorts

Its website is slow on purpose and makes you slow down before booking. I wanted an equally calm hero.

✓ I take it

Rapha

Very strong editorially, but it feels cold, like a closed club. It lacks warmth.

✕ I avoid it

I also looked at Snow Peak, Outlier, Issey Miyake, Loro Piana, Headspace, Muji and Tea Drunk.

Narrative

Messaging pillars

Three verbs that sum up what I want the page to make visitors feel. Recognise is about the person, prove is about the product, and invite is the call to action, which always goes hand in hand with one of the other two.

The landing page

Quiet garments for quiet miles

The home page has six blocks that alternate brand and sales. Each one activates one or two pillars, and none asks you to buy without first giving you a reason.

01 / 06 · Recognise · Invite

Hero

Before showing any product, the hero says who the brand is for: For those who run without a finish line. It has two buttons, one to shop and one to learn about the philosophy.

02 / 06 · Recognise

Who it's for

The weight of a fabric, a seam against the skin. This is the block where I want people to think this is for them.

03 / 06 · Prove · Invite

26 Comb collection

Nine garments, and each product card says where the fabric comes from (IT·01, merino). I took it from Aesop: the fact itself is the argument.

04 / 06 · Prove

Fabric with memory

First the fabric in close-up, then the garment. Naming the places (Lombardy, Kyoto, Lyon) proves the origin story is serious.

05 / 06 · Recognise · Invite

Manifesto

The manifesto closes the brand section on a black background. The buttons come back, but here they build on everything you've already read.

06 / 06 · Invite

Newsletter and footer

A calm invitation to stay, with no discounts or urgency, in the same tone as the rest of the page.

Process

AI as a tool

I used it at three specific moments, to challenge ideas or move faster. The decisions were mine.

Claude

Challenge

I put my brand axes to it to see if they held up. We agreed on the Identity and Minimalist pair, and it helped me refine the attributes.

Figma AI

Image generation

The product and lifestyle photos are AI-generated. The exercise focused on narrative, so it didn't make sense to spend time on photography.

Figma Sites

Publish

I turned the mid-fi into a browsable site to review the rhythm, the scroll and the image-to-text balance as a real visitor would see it.

Interface

Quiet luxury

The whole visual world comes from one idea: quiet luxury. What's good doesn't need to shout; you notice it in the material, the spacing and the rhythm.

Concept

Fewer, better choices

Lots of white, warm photography and soft transitions. As with Aman or Issey Miyake, the product has room to breathe and nothing fights for attention.

Foundations

The system

Cormorant Garamond for headings and DM Sans for body text. Black, greys and white, with a single orange accent (#F54E00) that sets the rhythm. Straight grids, no rounded corners.

Iteration

From the class version to v2

In class we made a first wireframe in 45 minutes. Over the following weeks I redid it from scratch, with more time and better judgement.

Before · Class wireframe

After · v2

Pillars

Verbs that do something

Identify, validate and drive became recognise, prove and invite. That way they describe what the page does to the visitor.

✓ v2

Modules

Fewer blocks

I went from eight blocks to six: I removed the testimonials and the philosophy section, and merged the newsletter with the footer. The page breathes more.

✓ v2

Language

Copy in English

I moved all the copy to English and kept polishing it until every sentence sounded calm and restrained, like the brand.
